# Old Dreams and Aftershocks: Reviewing the Logic of Trump's Past Visits to China

From 2017 to 2025, Trump set foot on Chinese soil four times. On the surface, it appeared to be a businessman reconnecting with an "old friend." Beneath the facade lay the essence of great power rivalry. This article reviews each visit and attempts to decode the strategic logic behind them.

## The First Visit (November 2017): "The Art of the Deal" Put to Test

Just ten months into his presidency, Trump embarked on his five-nation Asia trip, with Beijing as a key stop.

**Key Outcomes:**
- $250 billion in trade and investment deals (framework agreements)
- Establishment of four high-level dialogue mechanisms
- The North Korea issue dominated the agenda

**The Logic:**

The businessman-turned-president approached diplomacy as a transaction. He needed China's cooperation on North Korea, and China's "window period" for managing the nuclear crisis aligned conveniently with Trump's political urgency to show achievements.

> "I like negotiating with China from a position of strength — but I also know that without China's help, the North Korea problem cannot be solved."  
> — Trump's private remarks to reporters aboard Air Force One on his return flight

---

## The Second Visit (June 2019): A "Temporary Ceasefire" in the Trade War

At the G20 Osaka Summit, the US and China had just concluded a fierce round of tariff escalation. Trump met with the Chinese leader and announced a "pause on new tariff hikes."

**Key Context:**
- In May 2019, the US imposed 25% tariffs on $200 billion worth of Chinese goods
- American agricultural states suffered heavily from China's retaliatory measures (soybean exports dropped 75%)
- Campaign season for the 2020 election was about to begin

**Analysis:**

This visit was essentially a political operation targeting the farm vote. Trump needed to project an image of "able to negotiate" on China while appeasing constituents in agricultural states bearing the costs of the trade war. China's post-meeting commitment to purchase more American agricultural products came at precisely the right moment.

## The Third Visit (2023?): A Business Trip After Leaving Office

Strictly speaking, this was a "former president" rather than "sitting president" visit, yet the information leak from this commercial activity exceeded the norm.

**What Happened:**

Trump, as the Mar-a-Lago host, received a Chinese business delegation. Reports indicate the two sides discussed "potential joint venture projects," but details were never made public.

**Notable Details:**

After the visit, Trump posted on Truth Social: "The Chinese leader wrote me a very friendly letter." The content of this letter was never disclosed.

## The Fourth Visit (2025): A Probe in Tariff War 2.0

This was the most closely watched visit. Tariff War 2.0 had been raging for months, and both sides were looking for breathing room.

**Background Before the Visit:**
- Average US tariffs on Chinese goods exceeded 40%
- China's countermeasures targeted American agricultural products and energy imports
- Global supply chains remained under sustained pressure
- Both sides needed a "pause button"

**Core Agenda:**
1. Specific conditions for tariff relief
2. Wording of a "joint statement" on the fentanyl issue
3. Confirmation of "red lines" on Taiwan
4. Defining boundaries of technology export controls

**The Logic:**

Trump's negotiation strategy has always been "extreme pressure first, then negotiate at the table." The visit itself was not the goal — "making tariffs into negotiating chips" was the goal. His pre-departure statement, "I won't make a deal unless the terms are good," was textbook opening rhetoric.

## Aftershocks: Reading the Underlying Logic of US-China Relations from These Visits

### 1. Issue Linkage

Trump's team, especially during his first term, excelled at bundling different issues for negotiation: North Korea cooperation in exchange for trade concessions, technology controls in exchange for market access. But this strategy is losing effectiveness — China no longer views "accommodating America" as self-evident.

### 2. Personal Relations vs. Institutional Constraints

Trump favors the "strongman-to-strongman" approach, believing that personal chemistry with the Chinese leader can transcend differences. However, events like the 2019 Hong Kong protests and the 2022 Pelosi Taiwan visit prove that individual "friendly relations" cannot withstand structural contradictions.

### 3. The Double-Edged Sword of Tariffs

Tariffs are Trump's biggest bargaining chip and his biggest vulnerability. Tariffs at 40% are ultimately paid by American consumers, and inflation pressure hangs over Trump like a "Sword of Damocles."

### 4. The Absence of Multilateral Frameworks

All visits were "bilateral deals" lacking anchor in multilateral rules. This means every agreement reached is followed by even fiercer friction.

---

## Conclusion: Old Dreams Hard to Renew, Aftershocks Unending

Trump's visits to China, on the surface, appeared to be interactions between a "businessman president" and an "old friend." In reality, they were microcosms of America's transition from "engagement and cooperation" to "competition and containment" toward China.

The "honeymoon period" after each visit is shortening. The effectiveness of each "ceasefire agreement" is fading. This is not personal failure — it is the inevitable result of structural contradictions.
