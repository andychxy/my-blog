It was 2009 at the University of Waterloo, Canada. A student raised a hand during the Q&A, voice laden with indignation:

> *"In your speech, you've made many references to specific people — especially some in your audience — as Nazis. That is extremely offensive. Those people are German, and they suffered terribly under Nazi rule. I don't respect that anymore. I really don't."*

Norman Finkelstein did not flinch. He stood up and delivered a response that has been widely quoted ever since.

<!--more-->

## "My Late Father Was in Auschwitz": A Family History That Cannot Be Silent

Finkelstein's response was to lay his family's trauma on the table:

> *"I don't like and I don't respect this crocodile's tears. I very much dislike invoking the Holocaust in front of an audience, but this time I have no choice."*

He continued:

> *"My late father was in Auschwitz. My late mother was in Majdanek. Every single member of both sides of my family was executed. My parents both participated in the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising — in 1943, when the Germans razed the entire ghetto to the ground."*

This was not abstract history. Finkelstein's parents were survivors and witnesses of the Holocaust. His entire extended family was nearly wiped out in one of the darkest chapters of human history.

It was from this upbringing that he derived his moral conviction:

> *"It was precisely because of how my parents raised my two brothers and me that I refuse to stay silent in the face of Israel's crimes against the Palestinian people!"*

## The Instrumentalization of Holocaust Memory: A Decades-Long Critical Theme

The core of Finkelstein's response was not merely a rebuttal of a personal attack. It was a theme he had spent his academic career反复论证 — **the memory of the Holocaust has been instrumentalized by Israel and its supporters to silence criticism of Israeli policy and to provide moral cover for the occupation of Palestinian territories.**

His most famous work, *The Holocaust Industry: Reflections on the Exploitation of Jewish Suffering* (2000), argued explicitly that Israel and its lobby had converted the memory of the European Holocaust into a political instrument. Whenever the international community criticized Israeli occupation policies, accusations of anti-Semitism would follow, and any criticism of Israeli policy was equated with hatred of Jews.

The consequence: the genuine suffering of Holocaust survivors was being used to legitimize new injustices. Finkelstein believed this was not just morally odious — it was a betrayal of history itself.

His response at Waterloo was the most personal expression of this conviction.

## "Weep for the Palestinians": Reversing the Moral Discourse

In the final part of his response, Finkelstein delivered the most widely quoted passage:

> *"In my view, nothing is more despicable than using their [the Jewish people's] suffering and sacrifice to legitimize the torture, violence, and territorial seizure that Israel commits against the Palestinian people every single day."*

> *"I will not accept your attempt to moralistically blackmail and intimidate me with tears. If you have any shred of compassion left, you should be weeping for the Palestinian people."*

The power of these words lay in their refusal to accept a deeply entrenched hierarchy of discourse: not everyone who speaks for Palestine is "anti-Semitic," and those who politicize Holocaust memory are the ones who truly betray it.

## About Norman Finkelstein

Norman Finkelstein is a renowned American political scientist, author, and public intellectual, born in 1954 in New York to a Jewish family. Both his parents were Holocaust survivors, and this background profoundly shaped his academic and political trajectory.

**Academic Background:**
- BA from Princeton University, 1975
- PhD in Political Science from Harvard University, 1980
- His doctoral dissertation formed the basis of *The Holocaust Industry*

**Major Works:**
- *The Holocaust Industry: Reflections on the Exploitation of Jewish Suffering* (2000) — the book that made him both famous and infamous, earning him a reputation as a "controversial scholar"
- *Beyond Chutzpah: On the Misuse of Anti-Semitism and an Academic Freedom* (2005) — further critiqued the Israeli lobby's erosion of American academic freedom
- *Gaza: An Inquest into the Martyrdom* (2021) — documented systematic harms in the Gaza Strip

**Core Positions:**
Finkelstein's central argument is that Israel's invasion and occupation of Palestinian territories violates international law, and that the label of "anti-Semitism" is weaponized to suppress debate about Israeli conduct. He has consistently maintained that criticizing the actions of the Israeli state is not equivalent to hatred of Jews — the two are fundamentally different.

**Later Years:** Finkelstein's outspoken positions led to ongoing marginalization in academia. In 2007, the University of Notre Dame declined to renew his contract. In 2008, his tenure review at Hunter College in New York was also obstructed. Nevertheless, he never stopped writing and speaking out, publishing his most recent book on Gaza in 2021.
